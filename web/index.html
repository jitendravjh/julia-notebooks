The shape of the world

Real coastlines morphing continuously between projections, then Tissot's indicatrix showing exactly what each one gives up. Mercator keeps every circle a circle and lets them grow without limit. Gall–Peters keeps every area and wrecks every angle. Robinson keeps neither, on purpose.

A round-trip atlas

Project a point, convert it back, measure how far it moved. Do that everywhere and the errors turn out to have structure: Robinson's fall in bands on the five degree spacing of its lookup table, Transverse Mercator's in two discs at its singularity.
